# Instagram Strategy

Social is the bigger lever for a business this size — SEO gets you found
when someone's already searching; Instagram is what makes someone start
searching in the first place, and it's where "authentic Italy" travelers
actually spend their discovery time (over generic travel blogs or ads).
This doc is a content system you can run without a huge time budget: what
to post, how often, and in what voice.

## Before anything else: the Why-How-What order

Per `BUSINESS_PLAN.md` §8 — every caption should lead with the belief, not
the itinerary:

1. **Why:** Travel has become a performance — watching a version of local
   life staged for tourists. People are hungry for the opposite: to
   actually belong somewhere for a few days.
2. **How:** Small groups (6–10), real relationships with real people —
   Maria Grazia, Davide, Emiliano — not actors or staged demonstrations.
3. **What:** Small-group trips in Blera — olive harvest, sauce day,
   cheesemaking, Etruscan tombs, meals with local families.

If a caption opens with "Join us for an olive harvest!" before it
establishes why that matters, rewrite it. Lead with belief, land on
activity.

## Content pillars (rotate through these, don't post the same type twice in a row)

1. **Process, not posed** — hands actually harvesting, actually curing
   meat, actually working the press. Unpolished beats staged here; the
   whole brand promise is "not a performance."
2. **The people** — Maria Grazia, Davide, Emiliano, Nicolò — short
   profiles, their story, why they still do this work. This is content
   competitors literally cannot copy, because it depends on relationships
   Rasna actually has.
3. **The place** — Blera's rock-cut tombs, the Via Clodia, the hill town
   itself, golden-hour shots of the countryside. Establishes "this is a
   real, ancient place," not a generic countryside backdrop.
4. **Proof / social proof** — guest photos and quotes once you have pilot
   groups. This is your highest-converting content type once it exists;
   until then, lean harder on pillars 1–3.
5. **Practical / FAQ** — "What's actually included," "How is this
   different from a bus tour," pricing transparency. Short, direct,
   answers an objection.

## Format guidance

- **Reels > static posts** for reach — Instagram's algorithm still
  favors video substantially. Even a 10-second clip of hands pressing
  olives outperforms a polished flat-lay.
- **Carousels** work well for pillar 2 (people) and pillar 5 (FAQ) — a
  slow reveal (photo → quote → activity tie-in) holds attention longer
  than a single image.
- **Stories** for real-time behind-the-scenes during an actual trip or
  pilot — lower production bar, higher intimacy, and a good place to
  push the "link in bio" to book.

## Posting cadence (realistic for a solo operator)

- **2–3 feed posts/Reels per week** — better to post consistently at this
  rate than burn out trying for daily.
- **Stories whenever something is actually happening** — no fixed
  cadence, just capture in the moment.
- Batch content when you can: a single afternoon in Blera with Maria
  Grazia or during an activity can produce a week or two of posts if you
  shoot more than you think you need.

## Hashtag strategy

Keep it tight (8–15 tags, not 30) and mix specificity:

**Niche/specific** (low competition, high intent):
`#Tuscia` `#Blera` `#EtruscanItaly` `#TusciaItalia` `#ViaClodia`

**Category** (medium competition):
`#SmallGroupTravel` `#SlowTravel` `#AuthenticItaly` `#ItalyOffTheBeatenPath`
`#AgriturismoItaly`

**Broad reach** (high competition, use 2–3 max):
`#ItalyTravel` `#VisitItaly` `#ItalyTrip`

## Sample captions (Why → How → What order)

**Olive harvest post:**
> Most people who visit Italy watch someone else make olive oil. Here,
> you're the one up the ladder.
>
> We built Rasna around one idea: travel only changes you when you stop
> being a spectator. So every group is capped at 6–10 people, working
> alongside the families who actually farm this land — not a staged
> demo timed for a tour bus.
>
> This is olive harvest week in Blera, Tuscia — 90 minutes from Rome,
> 2,800 years of history, zero tour buses. Link in bio to join a
> departure or build your own dates.

**People/Maria Grazia post:**
> Maria Grazia has lived in Blera her whole life. When we started Rasna,
> she made one thing clear: this isn't a performance for tourists — if
> you're going to be at her table, you're doing the work first.
>
> That's the whole model. Real hosts, real work, real meals — not
> actors, not a script. Comment "BLERA" and we'll send you the full
> guide to what a trip here actually looks like.

**FAQ/objection post (carousel):**
> "How is this different from a normal Italy tour?"
>
> Most tours move 20–40 people through sights by bus, on a fixed script.
> Rasna caps every group at 6–10 people and builds the trip around real
> local life — you work alongside the people who actually farm the land,
> cure the meat, and cook the meals. €1,450/person, all-inclusive:
> lodging, all meals, every activity, transport from Rome or Viterbo.
> No hidden extras. Link in bio for the next departure.

## What ties back to the website

Every post's link-in-bio should point at `rasnaexperience.com`, and once
you're posting regularly, it's worth linking to the specific guide pages
for relevant content:
- A tombs/history post → `rasnaexperience.com/etruscan-tombs-guide.html`
- A "why small group" post → `rasnaexperience.com/small-group-italy-tours.html`
- A regional/day-trip post → `rasnaexperience.com/tuscia-travel-guide.html`

This is a small flywheel: Instagram content drives traffic to the guide
pages, the guide pages are built to rank in search independently, and both
funnel back to the booking section.
