# Instagram Strategy

Social is the bigger lever for a business this size — SEO gets you found
when someone's already searching; Instagram is what makes someone start
searching in the first place, and it's where "authentic Italy" travelers
actually spend their discovery time (over generic travel blogs or ads).
This doc is a content system you can run without a huge time budget: what
to post, how often, and in what voice.

## Before anything else: the Why-How-What order

Per `BUSINESS_PLAN.md` §8 — every caption should lead with the belief, not
the itinerary:

1. **Why:** Travel has become a performance — watching a version of local
   life staged for tourists. People are hungry for the opposite: to
   actually belong somewhere for a few days.
2. **How:** Small groups (6–10), real relationships with real people —
   Maria Grazia, Davide, Emiliano — not actors or staged demonstrations.
3. **What:** Small-group trips in Blera — olive harvest, sauce day,
   cheesemaking, Etruscan tombs, meals with local families.

If a caption opens with "Join us for an olive harvest!" before it
establishes why that matters, rewrite it. Lead with belief, land on
activity.

## Content pillars (rotate through these, don't post the same type twice in a row)

1. **Process, not posed** — hands actually harvesting, actually curing
   meat, actually working the press. Unpolished beats staged here; the
   whole brand promise is "not a performance."
2. **The people** — Maria Grazia, Davide, Emiliano, Nicolò — short
   profiles, their story, why they still do this work. This is content
   competitors literally cannot copy, because it depends on relationships
   Rasna actually has.
3. **The place** — Blera's rock-cut tombs, the Via Clodia, the hill town
   itself, golden-hour shots of the countryside. Establishes "this is a
   real, ancient place," not a generic countryside backdrop.
4. **Proof / social proof** — guest photos and quotes once you have pilot
   groups. This is your highest-converting content type once it exists;
   until then, lean harder on pillars 1–3.
5. **Practical / FAQ** — "What's actually included," "How is this
   different from a bus tour," pricing transparency. Short, direct,
   answers an objection.

## The "No Tourists Italy" series

Started with the first Blera-streets Reel (silent walk, Manu Chao audio,
opening on-screen text "Welcome to No Tourists Italy"). That opening line
is strong enough to be a recurring device, not a one-off — reuse it as the
fixed opening text/hook on every Reel in this style so the account becomes
recognizable before someone reads the caption. Caption should always open
with the same line, verbatim, so what's read matches what was just seen:

> Welcome to No Tourists Italy.
>
> [1–2 lines of Why — what makes this moment/activity not a performance]
>
> [What — the concrete place/activity/person, tied back to Blera/Tuscia
> facts: 90 min from Rome, 2,800 years of Etruscan history, ~3,000 people,
> zero tour buses]. Link in bio if you want to [do/see] it too.

Rotate the *why* across pillars so the series doesn't repeat itself:
- Streets/place → "the second you stop touring a place and start
  wandering it, it stops being a backdrop"
- Olive harvest → "most people watch someone else make olive oil; here
  you're the one up the ladder"
- Davide/cheesemaking → "no demo timed for a tour bus"
- Tombs/Via Clodia → "2,800 years old and nobody roped it off for you"

**Not every post is a "No Tourists Italy" post** (clarified 2026-08-19,
Nikolai's call). Reserve the fixed "Welcome to No Tourists Italy" opener
for the recurring Reel format it's actually tied to — don't force it onto
every caption by default. Plenty of posts (the sauce-day post, most
carousels) work better as a standalone caption in the same Why→How→What
voice without the series hook. Use judgment: if it's part of the
recognizable Reel style, use the opener; if it's a one-off caption for a
different kind of post, write it standalone like the sauce-day example in
the content queue below.

## Visual identity / cover system

The grid needs a consistent look, not just a consistent philosophy. Rule:
**every cover is one activity or one person, never generic scenery**, and
alternate "the place" (streets, tombs, landscape) with "the
people/process" (hands working, a host's face) so the grid doesn't read as
all-scenery or all-food.

**Color grade** (apply to every cover/thumbnail): warm, slightly
desaturated golden-hour grade pulling toward the site's palette —
`#A8AD6E` (olive), `#E5D8C2` (tufo stone), `#4E1A26`/`#6B2436` (bordeaux).
Avoid cool blue-sky, oversaturated "travel blog" HDR — that's the generic
look every Tuscany competitor already has. One saved CapCut/editing preset
applied consistently beats a more "sophisticated" grade redone each time.

**Cover text treatment**: small serif caption-style text (Cormorant
Garamond or closest match), cream/gold, sitting directly on the photo —
no boxes, no bars, no bold sans headline. This is the permanent look for
the "Welcome to No Tourists Italy" line — same position/size/color every
time.

**Reusable AI cover-image prompt** (placeholder only — swap in a real
frame from actual footage the moment one exists; "the place"/"the people"
pillars live or die on being real, not generated):

> A realistic, cinematic photograph (not illustration) of [ACTIVITY] in
> Blera/Tuscia, Italy. Golden hour light, warm and low. Color grade: muted
> sandy cream, olive green, terracotta, deep bordeaux, dark umber —
> desaturated, not vivid. Documentary handheld feel, natural, unstaged, no
> posed smiling-at-camera tourists, no modern signage, no logos. Vertical
> 9:16, cinematic depth of field, natural film grain.
> Overlay small serif text near the top third, warm cream/gold,
> tracked-out letterforms (style: Cormorant Garamond), reading exactly:
> "WELCOME TO NO TOURISTS ITALY" — no box, no bar, sitting directly on the
> photo. No other text.

`[ACTIVITY]` examples drawn from the confirmed roster in
`BUSINESS_PLAN.md`: hands pressing olives at an olive mill · a hand-cut
wheel of cheese and cured meat on a wooden board, Civitella Cesi · a
narrow Etruscan rock-cut tomb entrance carved into tufo rock · an empty
stone street at golden hour · a weathered hand slicing homemade
charcuterie.

### Phone-candid variant (no faces, group visible)

For process shots that need to look like they were actually taken on-site
with a phone (not a staged AI render), and where several people are
working at once: use the same color-grade/no-signage/no-logo rules above,
but swap the "cinematic photograph" framing for this:

> A candid photo that looks like it was taken on an iPhone, not a
> professional camera — slightly imperfect framing, natural handheld
> angle, realistic phone-camera dynamic range. [SCENE]. Two or three
> people [doing ACTIVITY], shown only from the shoulders down or from
> behind — no faces visible anywhere in frame. Color grade: warm,
> slightly desaturated golden tones — muted sandy cream, olive green,
> terracotta, deep bordeaux. No modern signage, no logos, no posed
> smiling. Vertical 9:16, natural phone-photo grain, not overly sharp.

Used for the sauce-day sequence (crates → boiling → smashing → bottling →
finished bottles) — reuse this pattern for any future multi-person process
shoot (olive press, cheesemaking) where a phone-shot, editorial-not-staged
look matters more than a single cinematic hero frame.

### Image sourcing — check before using any photo

Two things to verify before a photo goes anywhere near a branded post,
learned the hard way scouting cover images:

1. **Ownership.** Don't use a photo pulled from a search/blog/tour-site
   result, even if it's the perfect shot — check for a watermark and
   assume no license unless you shot it yourself or have explicit
   permission. A watermarked or scraped photo under a paid booking CTA is
   a real infringement risk, not just a look problem.
2. **Color grade.** Confirm it matches the warm golden-hour grade above
   before posting — a cool blue-sky/HDR shot (common in generic
   "Tuscany hilltown" stock/tourist photos) reads as exactly the generic
   competitor look this account is trying to avoid. If a real photo you
   want to use is graded wrong, warm it up first:
   > Adjust the color grading of this photo to a warm, slightly
   > desaturated golden-hour look — pull blue sky toward warm cream/gold
   > tones, mute greens toward olive, reduce overall saturation and
   > contrast slightly for a documentary, filmic feel. Do not change the
   > composition, content, or add/remove anything — grading only, same
   > photo.

### Gemini quality-enhance prompt (for real phone photos)

For actual footage/photos shot on-site that just need cleaning up — no
retouching, no content changes, just definition:

> Enhance the technical quality of this photo without altering its
> content in any way. Increase sharpness and fine detail, improve
> resolution/upscale it, reduce noise and compression artifacts, and
> correct slight exposure/white balance issues if needed. Do not retouch,
> edit, remove, add, or move any object, person, or element. Do not
> change the composition, cropping, colors/color grade, lighting mood, or
> style. The result should look like the exact same photo, just clearer
> and higher-definition — as if it were shot on better camera hardware,
> not stylized or filtered.

## Format guidance

- **Reels > static posts** for reach — Instagram's algorithm still
  favors video substantially. Even a 10-second clip of hands pressing
  olives outperforms a polished flat-lay.
- **Carousels** work well for pillar 2 (people) and pillar 5 (FAQ) — a
  slow reveal (photo → quote → activity tie-in) holds attention longer
  than a single image.
- **Stories** for real-time behind-the-scenes during an actual trip or
  pilot — lower production bar, higher intimacy, and a good place to
  push the "link in bio" to book.

### Closing branded slide — carousels only, not Reels

**Reels:** no end card. Reels are judged on completion rate; a static CTA
slide at the end just gives people a reason to swipe/scroll away before
it loops. The CTA lives in the caption ("link in bio").

**Carousels:** a closing branded slide is worth adding — it's where
someone who's read the whole post decides to act. Keep it in the same
restrained visual language as everything else (no bold sans headline, no
colored box/bar, no logo lockup — that's the generic travel-agency look
this account is built to avoid):

- Background: a real Blera/Tuscia place shot (tomb, street, landscape) in
  the standard warm golden-hour grade — not a separate "graphic"
  background. Must pass the sourcing check above (yours or licensed).
- Text: small serif (Cormorant Garamond), cream/gold, sitting directly on
  the photo, positioned in the calmest area of the frame (sky, plain
  wall) — no box, no bar. Same treatment as the "Welcome to No Tourists
  Italy" line.
- Content, minimal, kept current with whatever departure is actually
  open:
  > RASNA
  > Blera, Tuscia — 90 min from Rome
  > Next departure: [dates] · Link in bio

## Posting cadence (realistic for a solo operator)

- **2–3 feed posts/Reels per week** — better to post consistently at this
  rate than burn out trying for daily.
- **Stories whenever something is actually happening** — no fixed
  cadence, just capture in the moment.
- Batch content when you can: a single afternoon in Blera with Maria
  Grazia or during an activity can produce a week or two of posts if you
  shoot more than you think you need.

## Hashtag strategy

Keep it tight (8–15 tags, not 30) and mix specificity:

**Niche/specific** (low competition, high intent):
`#Tuscia` `#Blera` `#EtruscanItaly` `#TusciaItalia` `#ViaClodia`

**Category** (medium competition):
`#SmallGroupTravel` `#SlowTravel` `#AuthenticItaly` `#ItalyOffTheBeatenPath`
`#AgriturismoItaly`

**Broad reach** (high competition, use 2–3 max):
`#ItalyTravel` `#VisitItaly` `#ItalyTrip`

**For Reels specifically, trim to 5** — pick one or two from each tier
above rather than stacking all three. Reels get judged on completion rate
more than hashtag reach, and a shorter, tighter set reads less spammy under
the caption. Default 5 for the "No Tourists Italy" series:
`#Tuscia` `#Blera` `#EtruscanItaly` `#SlowTravel` `#ItalyTravel`

## What ties back to the website

Every post's link-in-bio should point at `rasnaexperience.com`, and once
you're posting regularly, it's worth linking to the specific guide pages
for relevant content:
- A tombs/history post → `rasnaexperience.com/etruscan-tombs-guide.html`
- A "why small group" post → `rasnaexperience.com/small-group-italy-tours.html`
- A regional/day-trip post → `rasnaexperience.com/tuscia-travel-guide.html`

This is a small flywheel: Instagram content drives traffic to the guide
pages, the guide pages are built to rank in search independently, and both
funnel back to the booking section.
